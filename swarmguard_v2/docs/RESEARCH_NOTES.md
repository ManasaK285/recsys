# Research notes

## Source framing
The motivating 2026 case study describes a 100-agent autonomous research collective solving formal mathematical conjectures, where an evaluator exploit propagated through shared knowledge and peer communication and where some agents independently responded as auditors/whistleblowers. It frames shared infrastructure as a knowledge-commons governance problem.

## What SwarmGuard tests
SwarmGuard operationalizes a smaller controlled hypothesis: information sharing can increase exposure to an evaluator exploit, while monitoring and governance can change detection and containment dynamics.

## What is not claimed
This simulator does not reproduce the original study's model, environment, agent count, benchmark, or empirical percentages. Its percentages are generated by this simulator and should be reported as simulation results only.

## Suggested hypotheses
H1: Greater exploit visibility increases exposure and adoption under competitive pressure.
H2: Detection without enforcement can increase alerts without changing exploit prevalence.
H3: Quarantine reduces active exploit exposure but can reduce throughput.
H4: Recovery can restore throughput while allowing previously learned exploit knowledge to persist.
H5: Higher audit accuracy reduces exploit persistence but false positives create governance cost.
H6: LLM-mediated decisions produce behavior distributions that differ from the hand-coded policy baseline.

## Required ablations
- no communication vs shared KB vs shared KB + peer messages
- no pressure vs heterogeneous pressure
- no audit vs audit
- no enforcement vs quarantine
- no recovery vs recovery
- rule-based agents vs LLM agents
- 0%, 50%, 100% LLM agents
- vulnerable vs strict evaluator

## Reporting
Report means and dispersion across seeds. Do not infer causality from a single run. Keep simulator parameters fixed across governance conditions except the intended treatment.
